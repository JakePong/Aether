02 / The problem

Towers point down. Drones fly up.

12%→33%

of drone accidents caused by link loss, 2016 → 2022

StrongWeakNo linkDrone A, direct routeSignal at 90 m

Garzia & Stella, MDPI Computation (Sept 2026), 319 multirotor accidents · Qualcomm LTE drone trials (2017)

Cell towers aim their antennas down at people. Up at ninety meters, a drone hears many towers at once, and Qualcomm's drone trials measured worse signal quality at altitude because of interference from neighboring towers.

Watch drone A. It flies the direct route, measuring as it goes, and runs into a hole. Link lost, it flies home.

Across 319 drone accidents, link problems nearly tripled, from 12 to 33 percent.

04 / How it works

Measure. Map. Replan.

One flight helps the next.

  1. MeasureDrones log signal, delay and packet loss with position and altitude.
  2. MapA shared 3D map that other drones plan from.
  3. ReplanOnboard and live: reroute, change altitude or switch links when conditions change.
Drone A's pathDrone B planLive replanWeak or jammed

Software only, above PX4 or ArduPilot; the pilot's limits and failsafes stay in charge · Illustrative simulation, not a working controller

{A}: Here's the loop. Drone A's measurements, signal, delay and packet loss tagged with position and altitude, update a shared 3D map. Drone B plans from that map, so it never enters the hole.

{B}: Then it keeps checking its own live link. Watch: a jammer switches on. Drone B falls back to satellite, replans onboard and flies around it, and those new readings go back into the map. Our software sits above the autopilot, and the pilot's limits and failsafes always win.

On screen: Drone B (big, ringed) follows the blue planned route around the hole. Red rings pulse from a rooftop jammer; the bar reads JAMMED · SWITCHING TO SATELLITE, then SATELLITE BACKUP · REPLANNING, then REPLANNED · LINK HELD. The green tube is the live replan. This is the "Holdfast mode" idea, shown as part of Replan.

Today's options each solve one piece. Elsight's Halo adds a 93-gram box to every drone. uAvionix SkyLine manages radios but needs network setup. Dimetor sells coverage maps that still need something on the aircraft to act on them.

Aether is that piece: software that turns the map into a better flight path. And the space is real: Motorola paid 4.4 billion for drone-radio maker Silvus.

If asked "won't they build it?": they may cover parts already. We win a specific inspection workflow with easier integration and measured value, which we still have to prove.

We sell software only: 199 dollars per drone per month. It costs us about 30 to serve, for hosting, processing, map data and support, so we keep 169, an 85 percent gross margin.

A typical five-drone fleet pays about 12 thousand dollars a year. Our plan is two fleets in year one and 56 by year three: about 670 thousand dollars in revenue. These are our assumptions, not sales yet.

If asked: gross margin is not net profit. Year 1 fixed budget is $48,000 (build, sales, admin). Break-even needs about 25 average five-drone fleets at the Year 3 budget. Fleet counts are averages over each year.
